# The Ideal Day Exercise

The Ideal Day exercise helps you set goals and form a vivid picture of what you want to work toward. It touches on every area of life and helps you identify what matters most.

## Preparation

- Put away your phone and other distractions, and turn off notifications
- Get a pen and paper or open a text editor in full-screen mode
- Set aside an hour or two

## Ground rules

Write an essay describing your ideal day.

- **Make it an ordinary day, ideally a workday.** Leave out vacations and chores you only do because you have to, such as cooking, laundry, or clearing your inbox.
- **Imagine you have no constraints.** You have as much money as you want, can travel anywhere, and are in perfect health. Your family and friends are doing well. You can do whatever you want, wherever and whenever you like, with the people you choose.
- **Make it a day you could live over and over.** You should be happy to spend the rest of your life this way without getting sick of it or dying of boredom.

## Writing the essay

- Write a draft first, then revise it
- Imagine living through each moment and write in the present tense, with plenty of detail
- Keep it physically possible. For example, you can't teleport between the South Pole and the Mediterranean on the same day
- Use the questions below if they help, ideally in order

::: tip
When I write, I keep track of the time in the story. It helps me rein in my imagination and fit everything into one day.
:::

Pay attention to what you do and how you do it.

1. What do you see around you? What places and objects are there?
1. Who do you interact with?
1. What are you thinking and feeling?

## Questions to get started

### How does my ideal day start?

- Where do I wake up?
- What time do I wake up?
- What do I think and feel when I wake up?
- What do I do in the morning?
- What do I look like?
- What qualities do I have?
- Where do I live?
- What does my home look like inside and out?
- What do I think about?
- What do I talk about?
- Where do I have breakfast, and with whom?

### What do my ideal relationships look like?

- Am I in a relationship?
- Do I have children?
- Why do we love each other? What do our children value about us?
- What do we do together?
- What do we like about each other?

### What does my ideal workday look like?

- What do I do to fulfill my potential?
- What goals am I working toward?
- What kind of work do I do?
- What time do I start working?
- What do I do at work?
- What are my clients like?
- How does the first half of the day go?
- Where and with whom do I have lunch? What do we talk about?
- What am I wearing?
- What are my friends like?
- What do we talk about?
- How do my everyday tasks go?
- What do I think about on the way home?

### How does my ideal day end?

- What do I do in the evening? Who am I with?
- Where do I go to bed?
- What do I think about before falling asleep?
- Is anything worrying me?

## Afterward

- Take a picture of the page or save the file so you can read it in a few years
- Reread the essay. What small part of your ideal day could you try now? Start the morning with fresh juice? Sign up for yoga?
- Break the day down into parts and set goals to bring them to life
- Draw a mind map based on the essay to clarify what you want to work toward
- Describe your ideal day again in a month or a couple of years
- Revisit and revise the essay regularly as your dreams and goals become clearer
