# Résumé

A stronger résumé makes it easier to pass the initial screening and get an interview. That is its main job.

A complete résumé:

- Includes all the sections
- Accounts for all your experience
- Presents less relevant experience in a favorable light
- Uses enough keywords from job listings
- Is available in English and Russian

Make it easy to read.

- Use a standard structure.
- Keep it as brief as possible.
- Fit it on one A4 page.
- Cut filler, repetition, and generic phrases like “wrote code and fixed bugs.”
- Use clear, concise wording.
- Check spelling and typos.

Give it a readable layout.

- Use Word or Google Docs.
- Choose a simple design and avoid bright colors.
- Make sure it prints well in black and white.
- Save it as a PDF.
- Use a filename like `Arapov-FullStack-CV.pdf`.
- Check that text can be copied from the PDF intact.

::: tip
Ask a recruiter to review the finished résumé. A paid review is an option.
:::

## Basic information

Start with your full name and the role you want. Be as specific as possible. The rule of thumb here is to use `senior` if you have more than three years of experience and leave off `junior` or `mid-level` otherwise.

::: tip
Consider leaving off salary expectations. If you include a figure, use the market average.
:::

## Photo

When applying in Russia, you can include a professional photo that reflects how colleagues would see you at the office or on Zoom. On many international markets, an applicant photo may be unexpected.

## Contact information

Include your city and country, phone number, email address, and LinkedIn profile. Other links you can add include:

- GitHub
- Messaging apps such as Telegram
- Personal website and portfolio
- Social media
- Stack Overflow
- Medium
- CodeChef or HackerRank
- Habr Career profile

Leave out your age and date of birth.

::: tip
Keep email addresses and usernames simple. Check that linked profiles look presentable and use Latin script for your email display name.
:::

## Professional summary

This optional section introduces you as a professional in a couple of sentences. List your strongest points, then [distill them into roughly 50 words](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/#write-a-headline-for-your-resume-summary).

- Explain why the role suits you.
- Lead with your job title, for example, `Software Engineer...`.
- Mention your strengths.
- Use action verbs and the active voice.

If you are changing careers, explain why. Mention your interests and plans if they add to the strongest points you want a recruiter to see.

## Skills

- Group related skills for readability.
- Order them by relevance.
- List technical skills without proficiency ratings.
- For senior and more advanced roles, keep the list of technologies shorter.
- Include spoken languages here or in a separate section. Describe your proficiency in words such as `intermediate`.

Back up the skills you list in your projects, experience, or education sections.

## Projects

This optional section can cover personal projects, open-source contributions, freelance work, publications, and research.

For each project, include the name, your role, a one-sentence description, the technologies you used, a link to the live project, build, or PDF, and a GitHub link.

::: tip
To show freelance experience, pick two or three recent or substantial projects.
:::

## Work experience

For each employer, include:

- Organization and location
- Start and end dates
- Job title
- Responsibilities, skills, technologies, and achievements with measurable results

Account for gaps. Keep your account of your experience honest, and see [this discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FdoDJgfzC9c) for a different take. Keep entries short and leave extra detail on LinkedIn. Start with the most recent role. You can omit or shorten experience more than five years old. Give the duration in years and months to make it easier for recruiters. Freelance experience can receive extra scrutiny.

Highlight the following in order:

- Project scope and team size
- Whether you focused on front-end or back-end work
- Your contributions and leadership
- Business impact
- Relevance to the role you want

::: tip
Some international markets expect more metrics and numbers.
:::

## Education

List higher education, continuing education, courses, and certifications in reverse chronological order.

For each entry, include a brief description if needed, graduation year, and GPA if it is strong. Add relevant technologies, projects, research, awards, achievements, or skills.

::: tip
If you have multiple degrees, you can list the most recent or the most relevant.
:::

## Other interests

You can include additional skills, hobbies, and interests.
