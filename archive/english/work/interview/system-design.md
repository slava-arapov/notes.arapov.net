# System Design Interviews

In this round, you design a hypothetical system. It is difficult to do without practice.

## Preparation

[Egor Biriukov suggests](https://vas3k.club/post/15768/#Whiteboard-algoritmy) practicing these skills.

- Understand the problem.
- Use clarifying questions and explicit assumptions to establish scope and functional and nonfunctional requirements.
- Make rough estimates of load and technical requirements.
- Sketch a high-level architecture before diving into details.
- Identify the hardest part of the system and outline possible solutions.
- Explain the trade-offs aloud and clearly.

Practice fitting the process into 45–55 minutes, covering the topics in a consistent order, identifying what matters in a large problem, and taking the initiative.

To prepare:

- Study [Understanding Distributed Systems](https://www.amazon.com/Understanding-Distributed-Systems-distributed-applications/dp/1838430202).
- Consider reading [Designing Data-Intensive Applications](https://www.amazon.com/Designing-Data-Intensive-Applications-Reliable-Maintainable/dp/1449373321), the book with the boar on the cover.
- Take [Grokking the System Design Interview](https://www.educative.io/courses/grokking-the-system-design-interview).
- Read [System Design Interview – An Insider’s Guide](https://www.amazon.com/System-Design-Interview-insiders-Second/dp/B08CMF2CQF/ref=sr_1_2?crid=18KSHJQDMFKMT).
- Do a mock interview in the language you will use in the real interview.

A timed interview outline:

- *5–10 minutes* to clarify the problem, users, load, and other requirements
- *5 minutes* to estimate storage, network traffic, and other resources
- *10 minutes* to draw a high-level design with boxes and arrows and explain its goals, likely bottlenecks, and trade-offs
- *20 minutes* to dive deeper into selected parts of the system
- *5–10 minutes* to summarize the design, how it could scale, and how you would launch it

::: tip
Show your engineering judgment by discussing drawbacks and trade-offs and tying decisions to requirements. Keep specific technologies to a minimum, and mention relevant experience if it helps.
:::
