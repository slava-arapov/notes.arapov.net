# Marie Kondo’s Tidying Method

Marie Kondo is a consultant who helps people tidy their homes and clear out clutter. In *The Life-Changing Magic of Tidying Up*, she collects her advice and lays out a step-by-step method.

These are the ideas I found most useful. The book is worth reading for inspiration and to get into the right mindset.

The idea behind the KonMari method is to do one thorough tidy and see the results right away. After that, routine cleaning takes care of dust and dirt, and scattered belongings become a thing of the past. The space feels lighter, and so do you.

I tried the method for the first time when I lived in a dorm. In three days (15.5 hours), I gathered about ten big bags of clutter from a room of 14 square meters.

## How the KonMari method works

Kondo gives belongings an almost mystical significance. In her view, letting go of an object also lets you let go of a tie you no longer need. There is a practical side to this. Old belongings can bring back memories, negative associations, emotions, and thoughts.

Letting go of belongings that no longer bring you joy clears space at home and in your head.

## Before you start

People often report making big changes in their lives after tidying. Before you begin, try the following.

- Picture your ideal lifestyle. Write down how your space would change and what you would do in it.
- Ask yourself why you want to live that way and question what you wrote. Why do I want aromatherapy before bed? Why do I want to listen to classical music while doing yoga?
- Keep asking why until you get to the underlying reason.

Some goals may turn out to matter less than you thought, which can make it easier to let go of things.

## The two steps of the KonMari method

First, let go of everything that no longer brings you joy. Then give everything you keep a place.

### Step 1. Clear out what you no longer need

- Aim to finish the main tidy in a day or two. Seeing the difference all at once makes it easier to keep from slipping back into old habits.
- Work through one category at a time, gathering items from every room and cupboard.
- Clothes are usually the easiest place to start. Sort the whole category in one go and jot down a shopping list if it helps.
- Work your way from clothes to sentimental items. You can spread the categories over several days.

Go through the categories in this order.

1. Clothes
1. Books
1. Papers
1. Miscellaneous items
1. Sentimental items

Each category takes a little more judgment. By the time you reach sentimental items, you have had practice deciding what to keep.

Pick up each item and ask yourself:

> Does this spark joy?

Keep it if it does. Otherwise, let it go.

#### Clothes

1. Tops
1. Bottoms
1. Clothes that go on hangers
1. Socks
1. Underwear
1. Bags
1. Accessories
1. Clothing for specific activities
1. Shoes

You may find it easier to start with off-season clothes. You can also set them aside if you think you might need them.

Kondo suggests giving up worn-out clothes that have been demoted to loungewear. You can wear the same clothes at home as you would outside or get a set of dedicated loungewear.

##### Folding

Kondo recommends folding clothes into rectangles and storing them upright. Fold socks into rectangles too. They last longer when you avoid rolling them into balls.

#### Books

Books come into your life at a particular moment and serve a purpose. When a book has served its purpose, pass it on to someone who could use it or leave it on a book-exchange shelf.

#### Papers

Discard what you can and sort the remaining papers into three folders.

- Needs attention, like a Getting Things Done inbox
- Must keep, such as identification, contracts, and other important documents
- Worth keeping, such as warranties

Some documents can be scanned and stored in the cloud.

#### Miscellaneous items (komono)

- CDs and DVDs
- Skin care
- Accessories
- Valuables
- Electronics
- Household supplies
- Household stock
- Kitchen items
- Food
- Other items

#### Sentimental items

This seems like the hardest category. After ten hours or so of tidying, it goes much more smoothly.

::: tip
**What if something is hard to let go of?**

Take a moment to thank it. If you are still having trouble parting with it, ask yourself these questions.

- Why is this item hard to let go of? Am I attached to the past or worried about the future?
- Has it already served its purpose, helped me, or brought me joy?
:::

### Step 2. Give everything you keep a place

- Store items of the same kind together and keep storage areas close to one another
- Store items upright
- Use shoeboxes and containers to keep things organized

## Habits worth keeping

- Put things away as soon as you finish using them
- Empty your bag when you get home
- Cut down on visual noise by removing labels and other advertising clutter
